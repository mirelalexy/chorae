# Chorae

You know the feeling. Five playlists deep into *"cold winter nights"* — each one a slightly different attempt at the same ache, none of them quite it. A song stuck on repeat for three days and you don't fully know why or where to put it. What does it feel like? Where does it fit into your world? Maybe you have half-formed prompts in your head, maybe you've found a song that sets the mood for a playlist, but don't know where to find similar ones that strike you in the same way. Or maybe you want something new — a version of yourself you haven't heard yet.

Chorae is not a shelf to display your taste on, or another recap waiting for December, but a place that actually reasons about **why** you listen to what you listen to, helping you make something new out of it.

---

## Features

### Your taste, for real this time
Connect Spotify and see your top tracks and artists, your recently played, your saved albums, and curate a playlist of favorites, kept separate from whatever the algorithm thinks you're about this month. After all, your most played are not always your most loved. Chorae knows the difference.

### Describe it, get a playlist
Not a genre filter, but a conversation.

Maybe it's...

> *It's cold outside and I'm walking home from uni, nobody's waiting for me tonight. It's just me. I want something that feels like running through the city, letting myself be free. Feel it all. Maybe something between "Bad Omens" and "Red Desert" by 5SOS? Do you know any other songs that scratch that same itch?*

Or...

> *I'm trying to build a playlist for the main character for my book. He's a mercenary who's starting to remember why he stopped letting himself feel things. He's arrogant, charming, but also hopeless... completely exhausted. He is slowly starting to care about a woman he once hated. He feels confused, drained by his own misery and love. I need to feel that battle inside his brain. Feel what he's feeling, you know?*

Or...

> *I always feel like a zombie when I know I need to start cleaning up my room... I need something that makes starting feel less impossible, please.*

Or what about...

> *I want to feel like I'm walking through the RPD or an abandoned lab. Something that brings me closer to what Leon Kennedy is like in Resident Evil. I was thinking, something dark, dramatic, but also cool. Like "Psycho" by Muse, "Scissorhands" by Maggie Lindemann, "Impose" by Bad Omens, or "I Can Fix You" by Jutes? These are just some songs I kept listening to on repeat while playing the last RE game.*

A prompt shouldn't sound the same for everyone.

Chorae compares it against your actual taste and builds something grounded in real, playable tracks. Not a generic idea of "dark rock" or "sad pop," but something for **you**. If you notice it does not align with what you wanted, say so, and the next try will be better. Feedback matters.

### The cleanup you didn't know you needed
Five versions of "rainy days" or "chill nights" sitting in your library like unfinished thoughts?

Select the playlists that belong together, describe what they were always supposed to be, and let Chorae fold them into the one you actually meant to make.

### Meet Rae
Chorae, in-app, goes by Rae. And she doesn't just build playlists for you, she pays attention. She notices the songs you keep coming back to, the playlists you almost finished, the ones you keep trying to build but stop, and the artists that quietly become part of your month before you realize they have.

> *I don't technically have ears... but if I did, this one would've ruined them.*

Rae is just dramatic enough to make recommending music feel like introducing you to an old friend. And she'll absolutely make a terrible music pun if the opportunity presents itself. But she also knows when not to.

> *Different title. Same heartbreak?*

Rae notices patterns, not just moments. She cares more about the fifth time you've rebuilt the same playlist, the artist you quietly rediscovered after two years, or the one track you've played every morning this week. She talks about habits because that's where taste actually lives.

Do you notice weird album transitions, too? 

Rae does. She gets excited about finding out what you really like or notice. Songs are like tiny living things, and she likes making them live together.

> *Can I ask why you're suddenly into metal?*

She wants your library to make sense. She'll ask why you have six playlists about the same thing, notice recurring themes or genres, and remember your way of naming playlists.

## Ideas for the future

### Your listening journal
Leave thoughts after listening. Highlight favorite lyrics. Come back years later and remember exactly who you were when an album meant everything. Maybe even ask:

> *Why do I always return to heartbreak albums when I'm trying to move on? I keep listening to Olivia Rodrigo's "you seem pretty sad for a girl so in love" and it makes me feel worse...*

Or:

> *Why do hopeful songs suddenly feel uncomfortable?*

### Writing with Rae
Want to write something for yourself? Lyrics about something you went through? A person who hurt you? A moment you want to remember forever? Feel free to do so. Show Rae what you've written and ask what artists, writers, or songs it reminds her of — not to imitate them, but to better understand your own voice.
